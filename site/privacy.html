Privacy policy

Nothing to collect, nothing to share.

Calendar(s) privacy blur does not collect, store, sell or share any personal data. It has no servers, and nothing it does leaves your browser.

What the extension stores

  • Whether blur is on or off.
  • Your list of calendars: which built-in ones are enabled, and any site and selector you add in settings.

Both are kept in chrome.storage.local on your device. They are never sent anywhere, and removing the extension deletes them.

What it does not do

  • No network requests. No remote code, no analytics, no fetch or XHR of any kind. You can confirm this in DevTools → Network while toggling.
  • No page reading. The extension adds and removes a stylesheet that blurs event titles. Its content script only tells the extension that a calendar page has loaded; it never reads, extracts or sends page content.
  • No tracking. No cookies, identifiers, browsing history or usage statistics.

Permissions

  • storage, for the two settings above.
  • scripting, to add and remove the blur stylesheet.
  • Host access limited to the Outlook on the web calendar paths and calendar.google.com.
  • Any other https site you add in settings asks for its own permission at that point. You can revoke it from chrome://extensions at any time.

Changes and contact

Changes to this policy are published on this page and in the source repository. Questions go to GitHub issues.
